Ink 486 to 458. By code (all 30 parts): `annotation_ink_overlap` 30 to 23,
`annotation_outside_area` 280 to 257, `annotation_leader_crossing` 124 to 126,
`annotation_over_view` 15 to 15, `annotation_line_over_view` 37 to 37. Every other column is
unchanged. What changed, all in the callout leaders (`src/dims/leaders.rs`):

- **Text is protected.** A leader's crossings are counted per other item met and kind of contact
  (a line crossed; the label met by a line, a label, a leader or an arrowhead's flare), as the
  plan's collision checks report one problem per pair and code, not per segment; between as many
  crossings the candidate touching less text wins (draftwright protects text both ways,
  `annotation_text_ink_clear`). This is the `annotation_ink_overlap` fall; on some parts a label
  taken off a line costs its leader a crossing instead (+2 overall).
- **Ink inside the view's area is preferred** (below text, above length): callouts that stood a
  few millimetres past their view's bands move in (`annotation_outside_area` −23 net).
- **ISSUE-1187-a measured, not enforced:** `dims::clear_routes` sweeps a callout's leader from its
  sites (64 directions, ten reaches), after draftwright's test helper `_clear_routes`; on ctc04
  every retained crossing has no clear route (`tests/issue_regressions.rs`). As in draftwright,
  no placer runs the sweep. A post-assignment pass that moved blocked callouts onto sweep routes
  was tried (ink 449, −9 more: ctc02 −5, 10060 −2, ftc09's off-sheet callout onto its view) and
  removed in review, because draftwright has no such step (its Policy B retention follows from
  the assignment); bringing it back is a maintainer question (R4 decision 6 allows a repair step
  only allow-listed, idempotent and on strict improvement).

**One part is worse in the ink column: ctc03, 21 to 24. Question for the maintainer.** On each
fixed sheet this stream is no worse: A3 at 1:5 has ink 21 before and after (hard findings 3 to
1: ink overlaps 2 to 1, label overlap 1 to 0), A2 at 1:5 has 26 before and 24 after (hard
findings 1 to 0). But with A2 now valid and complete, the layout recovery (`model::plan_recovered`,
stream d-quality-off-sheet) takes A2 instead of keeping A3 with `layout_recovery_exhausted`, and
A2 at 1:5 has more linear dimensions past their estimated bands (`annotation_outside_area` 12 to
16, info) and one more leader crossing (7 to 8). By the plan's severities the drawing is better
(no hard finding); by the tool's ink count it is worse. Accept, or should the recovery weigh the
ink count? Not decided here; ctc03 is not one of the captured parts `tests/placement.rs` ratchets,
so no test records its residual.

**Not reached by this stream**, and why: most of the `annotation_outside_area` and all the
`annotation_line_over_view` and linear `annotation_over_view` are linear dimensions in lanes past
bands that are draftwright's estimates (`layout::Strips::reserve` reserves the port's lanes for
the side view only; the front's right ladder runs across the side view on ftc09, ftc10, cgb245),
which is the layout's (d-quality-view-overlap). A fewest-conflicts fallback for linear labels
along their lines (draftwright's `_InkConflictState.solve` descent) was tried and changed nothing
on these parts, so it was not kept. Gating text as draftwright does (any text contact ranked above
every crossing) was measured too, on an intermediate build: ink 506 against its 459, worse on ctc02 (+17),
ftc08 (+12), grm03 (+7). Question for the maintainer: keep the port's weighting (a text contact
is one more crossing, and the tie-break between as many), or gate text as draftwright does?
